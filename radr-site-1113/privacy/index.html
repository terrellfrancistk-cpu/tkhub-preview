Privacy

Last updated 4 November 2026

01 · Four things first

What is collected. Trip location is what RADR is for: while you record a drive, its position, speed and heading are written to your own phone. If you fill in the driver card, it can include health details such as blood type, allergies, conditions, medications and organ-donor status. Crash reports are sent on by default and carry the app version, OS version, device model and the failing code, and never an account or a location. If you share a live trip, the person following sees your first name, your position and your phone's real battery percent until the trip ends; you never see your own battery there, and the percent disappears when the trip stops.

Tracking is optional and off until you say yes. The RADR iPhone and Android apps run no ads, no ad identifier and no tracking SDK, and it never shows Apple's tracking prompt. Share usage data stays off until you tap Allow. If you say yes, once a day RADR sends that it was opened, the platform (iPhone or Android) and the app version, and nothing else. Turning it off in Profile stops it. Crash reports are separate and start on; Profile has a switch to turn them off.

How to delete your account. Open Account, tap Delete account, and type DELETE. On Android confirm with DELETE FOREVER. On iPhone confirm with Delete Account. That destroys the sign-in and everything synced to the account. The same steps, what is deleted or kept, and an email request if you cannot open the app are at https://radr-drive.rork.app/delete-account. Email support.radr@tkhub.app from the address on the account.

Support. Email support.radr@tkhub.app and a person reads it. This page loads without signing in, at radr-drive.rork.app/privacy.

02 · The short version

The RADR apps have no analytics SDKs and no ad networks. This website shows ads to visitors without a RADR Premium subscription, as described under Advertising on this website. Your drives, your reports, your encounter recordings and your ticket matters are written to storage on your own device and stay there. An account is optional — it exists only to carry your driver card, Signal balance and history between your own devices, and it never holds your recordings.

03 · What stays on your device

Trip telemetry — position, speed, heading and the posted limit, sampled once per second on a rolling 24-hour window.

Reports you submit and pins you confirm, along with your trust score and Signal balance. (A vote on a shared map pin while signed in is also sent to RADR; see What leaves your device.)

Encounter records from Officer Mode: the stopwatch log, quick tags, and any audio or video you chose to capture.

Ticket matters: the charge, citation and court details, the photographs you attach, the generated evidence brief, and the message thread with any practice you contact.

Your equipped theme, marker set, voice pack and app settings.

04 · How recordings are held

Trip logs, cases and the driver card are sealed on the phone with app-managed AES-GCM. On iPhone the key is in the Keychain. On Android the key is in the Android keystore. The PIN is not that key.

Encounter audio, video, clips and photos are files in the app's private storage. On iPhone, file protection makes those files unreadable while the device is locked. They are not sealed with the vault key. On Android they are not sealed with the keystore key either. The device lock protects them while the phone is locked.

Messages between drivers are end-to-end encrypted. Recordings, clips and photos are not.

05 · What leaves your device, and why

Voice search on Android. If you tap the microphone in the place search, your phone's own speech service turns what you say into text: on the device where your phone supports that, otherwise through the service your phone uses (usually Google's), under that service's terms. RADR receives only the text and uses it to search. RADR does not record or keep the audio.

Voice search on the web. If you tap the microphone in the map search on the RADR website, your browser asks for microphone access and its own speech service turns what you say into text: on your device where the browser supports that, otherwise through the service your browser uses, under that service’s terms. RADR receives only the text and uses it to search. RADR does not record or keep the audio.

Shared hazard reports. When you report a hazard from a real GPS fix, RADR sends it to nearby drivers: the kind of hazard, its position, the road name your phone found, the time, and a random identifier made for this phone when RADR was installed, used only so each phone counts once. Confirming or denying a shared report sends that same identifier and your vote. Reports expire on their own (most after 30 minutes; aircraft-enforcement reports after 30 days) and expired reports are deleted. A report made at the map centre, on a demo drive or without a GPS fix stays on your phone. With no signal, a report waits on your phone and is sent when you are back online.

Shared map pins. While the map is open, the app sends your current position to RADR's pin server so it can show pins near you. The server holds that position only while the connection is open and does not store it. If you are signed in and vote on a shared pin, the pin, your vote and your account are stored so that each account votes once on each pin.

Map tiles are requested from OpenFreeMap (OpenStreetMap and OpenMapTiles), Mapterhorn terrain, and EOX Sentinel-2 imagery. Location is also shared with OpenStreetMap services so limits, routes and search can work. Overpass receives the coordinates of a speed-limit lookup. Valhalla receives the origin and destination of a route. Nominatim receives the place you typed and a bias box around your current position. Where an agency feed is shown, your app requests that agency's published incident data from its open-data provider. These requests reveal the approximate area you are viewing to those providers, in the same way any map app does. They are not tied to an identity by us. The sources and their licences are listed in the app's map attribution.

Fixed speed, red-light and average-speed camera locations come from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database Licence (openstreetmap.org/copyright). RADR’s server fetches them by map area and caches them; your location is used only to pick the area and is not stored. Camera data can be incomplete or out of date; always obey posted limits.

An account is optional. With one, the driver card, Signal balance and trip history described under Your account are synced. Without one, those copies are not uploaded. Other features below send data only when you use them: ticket photographs, AI drafting, crash reports and a live share link you create yourself.

The alert pins you see and the attorney offers you receive are generated on your own device for demonstration. Submitting a report or sending a matter to the defence network does not transmit anything off the phone in this build — the app says so on screen, and we will not turn on a live network without saying so here first.

This website keeps one small security ledger for its owner dashboard: every sign-in attempt at the dashboard door is recorded with the time, the outcome, the city and country the network edge reports, and a hashed network id. No street address and no raw IP is ever stored or shown, and rows older than thirty days delete themselves. Nothing from the RADR apps is part of this ledger.

Crash reports are on by default. If RADR crashes or freezes on your phone, or this website hits a fault, a crash report is sent to RADR's own crash log so we can fix it: the app version, the operating system version, the device model (for example “iPhone16,1” or “Pixel 8”), the screen you were on, and the lines of code that failed. It carries no account, no device identifier, no location and nothing you typed; anything shaped like a coordinate or an email address is blanked before it is stored. It is not shared with any third party. Identical crashes are grouped into one row with a count, and rows untouched for thirty days delete themselves. Opt out in Profile under Send crash reports, on iPhone and Android. Off means nothing further is sent. Share usage data is a different switch and stays off until you opt in.

06 · Your account

An account is optional. RADR works fully without one, and signing out never deletes anything on your phone.

If you sign in, the app keeps in your account a copy of your driver card, your Signal balance and standing, and your trip and case history, so a new phone can pick up where the old one left off. Encounter recordings, clips and photographs never leave the phone that made them — not on sign-in, not on restore, not ever. Other preferences move between your devices only through a pairing code you generate yourself.

The sign-in stores the name and email your sign-in provider gives us. If you filled in a driver card or recorded trips while signed in, those are in the synced copy too, as the sections below describe.

If you ask RADR to text you a sign-in code, we send that code through Twilio to the mobile number you typed. That number is used only to deliver the code. It is not sold, not used for marketing, and not shared with anyone except Twilio, which delivers the text.

07 · Location

RADR requests location access because a speed readout and a proximity alert are impossible without it. Fixes are used to draw your position, compute speed, look up the posted limit and time-stamp your trip log. The speed-limit, routing and place-search lookups share that location with OpenStreetMap services, as described above. If you decline, the app still runs — it will synthesize a demonstration drive and label it DEMO DRIVE on screen so you are never misled about what you are looking at.

08 · Driver card health details

The driver card can hold health details you type: blood type, allergies, conditions, medications, organ-donor status and medical notes. That card syncs with the account when you are signed in. Health data you type is not sold and is not used for advertising.

09 · Drafting with AI

When you ask RADR to draft — a drive summary, a clip name, a stop account, a hazard report, a defence brief, or a radio-listening summary — the text of that job is sent through RADR's own AI gate to the model in use. That text can include location as a road name, a place, a distance, and the speeds and times on the drive. It is shared with the AI provider that drafts it. It does not include the raw GPS trace.

The engines the app can call are Grok (xAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), GPT (OpenAI), Llama (Meta), Mistral, DeepSeek and Qwen (Alibaba). Speech-to-text uses Grok's speech model. A vehicle portrait uses OpenAI's image model. A job uses the engines you selected, not the whole list every time. Questions about this go to support.radr@tkhub.app.

10 · Microphone and camera

These are used only inside Officer Mode, only while an encounter is armed, and only in the modes you configured. If a microphone or camera is unavailable or denied, the screen says so plainly and records telemetry only. Recording laws differ by jurisdiction; you are responsible for knowing yours.

11 · Attorneys and the defence network

RADR is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice, representation or an attorney-client relationship. When you retain a practice through RADR, that relationship, its fee agreement and its confidentiality obligations are between you and that practice. Communications with a practice are not privileged by virtue of passing through this app.

If you photograph a ticket and send it, RADR uploads that picture — a compressed JPEG of the citation — to TK Hub LLC so law firms can bid. The photograph, including the citation details printed on it, is shared with the law firms that bid on that ticket. A bid comes back with the firm name and the price. Firms that did not agree to refund their fee if the case is lost are not shown to you. The photograph is not used for advertising and is not shown to other drivers.

12 · This website

The house counts page views and live sessions. The owner dashboard is not public. The public widget is counts only.

That count records the time, the page, the referring site, a coarse device family such as iPhone or Windows, and the city and country the network edge reports — never a street address, and never a stored IP. The address your browser must send to reach any website is hashed with a salt that rotates daily, used only to cap automated floods, and nothing derived from it is written down. A session identifier is a random value your browser mints for itself and discards when the tab closes; it carries no name, email or account.

Rows older than seven days delete themselves. The feed is not sold, shared or fed to an advertising network, and no third-party analytics SDK runs on this site.

When you are signed in on this website, it also keeps an account activity log for the owner: the page you are on and the label of a button or form you use (for example “Tapped: Upload”), with the time, tied to your account. It never records what you type — no password, code, card number or message. It is used to see whether the site works for signed-in drivers and is not sold or shared. Signing out stops it. The RADR phone apps do not send this log.

13 · Advertising on this website

Public pages of radr-drive.rork.app may show ads served by Google AdSense to visitors who are not RADR Premium subscribers. Ads never appear on sign-in, account, payment or form pages, and the ad script is not loaded at all for signed-in Premium subscribers.

When an ad loads, Google and its partners may use cookies or similar identifiers to serve and measure ads, and, where the law allows and you have not opted out, to personalise them based on your visits to this and other sites. Our visit counter is separate and is never shared with Google. You can turn off personalised ads at adssettings.google.com or aboutads.info, and learn how Google uses this data at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Where required (for example in the EEA, UK and some US states), a consent choice is shown before personalised ads load.

The RADR iPhone and Android apps show no ads and contain no ad SDK.

14 · Children

RADR is intended for licensed drivers and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from them.

15 · Deleting your data

Removing the app removes what is stored only on the phone. Individual trips, encounters and matters can also be deleted from inside the app at any time. Deleting the account — Account, then Delete account, then type DELETE — destroys the sign-in and the synced copy (driver card, Signal, and history) at once. Recordings, clips and photos already on the phone are kept. Crash reports are not tied to the account and drop off after thirty days. A live ETA link expires on its own, never longer than twelve hours, or when you revoke it. Ticket photographs, purchase records and relayed message ciphertext sit outside the synced vault; email support.radr@tkhub.app and we delete those too. The page at https://radr-drive.rork.app/delete-account explains this without the app installed.

16 · Usage data

Sharing usage data is off until you turn it on. After the walkthrough on first run, RADR asks once: “Share usage data?” Decline, or closing the card, means no. You can change it any time in Profile under Share usage data.

While it is on, once a day RADR sends that the app was opened, the platform (iPhone or Android) and the app version. No account, no device id, no location, no drives and no recordings. Turning it off stops it.

We do not pay you, credit you or reward you for sharing, and nothing in the app is locked behind saying yes. The app works the same either way. No advertising network and no analytics SDK runs in the RADR apps, and nothing here tracks you across other companies' apps.
