# Curb - Data Retention and Disposal Policy

Version 1.3
Effective date: October 24, 2026
Owner: Founder and sole operator, Curb
Contact: support.curb@tkhub.app
Applies to: the Curb mobile application (iOS and Android) and the Curb backend service

## 1. Purpose and scope

Curb is a rideshare and food delivery application. This policy defines what personal data Curb collects, where it is held, how long it is kept, how it is destroyed, and how a consumer can have it deleted. It covers all data held by Curb, including data obtained through a legacy Plaid bank link, and it binds every system Curb operates.

This policy is enforced through the design of the product itself. The retention limits described below are implemented in Curb's code and in the configuration of its processors, not left to manual discretion.

## 2. Data classification

Curb classifies the data it handles into three tiers.

- Tier 1 - Sensitive financial data. Bank account and routing numbers, full card numbers, card security codes, Plaid access tokens, government identifiers. Curb no longer accepts typed-in bank account or routing numbers. Older driver records may still hold a hand-typed account entered before that change; it is deleted when the driver removes it or deletes their account, and it must be purged before payouts go live.
- Tier 2 - Personal data. Pickup and dropoff locations for an active job, masked payout account details, payment method references, driver email addresses, trip and payout records.
- Tier 3 - Non-personal data. App settings, saved place labels, fare configuration, and other data that is not tied to an identified person.

## 3. Where data is held

- On the user's own device. Saved places, driver documents, ratings and app settings are stored in the app's local storage on the device.
- Curb's backend. Operated on Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects, hosted in the United States. Records are keyed to the user's Curb account. An account is opened by signing in with an email code, a phone code, Apple or Google. Curb holds the sign-in email address or phone number, a verified order contact number, ride and payment records, and any rental or storage listing a user publishes, including its photos. Curb does not hold advertising identifiers.
- Processors. Stripe (card processing and driver payouts), Plaid (a legacy bank link kept only for drivers who linked before Stripe payouts; no new links are made), Resend (transactional email), Twilio (phone sign-in codes, masked recorded calls, and one text to a passenger someone else booked for), and Checkr (driver background checks on Checkr's own page). Each acts only on Curb's documented instructions. The in-app assistant forwards a message or a photo to our AI provider (TK Engine) and Curb does not store that message or photo.

## 4. Data Curb explicitly does not store

The following are never written to Curb's storage, logs, backups, or analytics, in production or in any test environment:

- Full card numbers, expiry dates or card security codes. Card entry occurs on Stripe's hosted checkout page. Curb receives only an opaque Stripe reference plus the card brand and last four digits.
- Bank login credentials of any kind. Payout bank details are entered with Stripe.
- Social Security numbers, dates of birth, and licence numbers used for a background check. Those are entered on Checkr's hosted page. Curb does not receive them and does not store them.

## 5. Retention schedule

Data is retained only as long as it serves the purpose it was collected for.

- Driver location during a live trip: latitude and longitude, and from an iPhone the phone's battery level, kept on that trip as the latest point and a trail of up to 240 points. New points are accepted only while the trip is accepted, arrived, or under way. The trip record, including that trail, is dropped from the dispatch board two hours after its last update. It is not kept as a long-term location history.
- A rider's background location reading for a booked ride: read on the phone and not uploaded.
- Safety-share location: the phone's position while a share is open. The share expires after six hours. Stopping it deletes the share about thirty minutes later.
- Masked-call recordings: kept 30 days, or 180 days if the call was reported, then deleted.
- Crash and launch reports: kept 30 days, and only after the person turns usage sharing on. A report has no name, email, phone, address, or coordinates.
- Curb Kids child details (first name, last initial, age, seat, note, receiving adult): kept on the parent's phone until the parent deletes the child. Curb's servers do not store them. The school calendar and an adult driver or companion certification are kept until the adult deletes the account or asks for deletion.
- Live pickup and dropoff coordinates for an active job: kept with that job and dropped with the dispatch board, two hours after the job's last update. Not sold or shared.
- Legacy Plaid access tokens: retained until Plaid confirms the bank link is removed (or reports it already gone). If Plaid cannot be reached, the token is kept and removal is retried; it is never dropped before Plaid has let go of the link.
- Legacy masked bank details (institution, subtype, last four): deleted together with the driver record.
- Payment method references (Stripe customer and payment method identifiers): retained only while the rider keeps a saved payment method. Deleted immediately on removal.
- Completed trip and payout records: retained up to seven years, as required by United States tax and financial record-keeping obligations, then deleted. This is the only category Curb retains on a long horizon, and it is retained solely to meet a legal requirement.
- On-device data: retained until the user clears it or deletes the app. Under the user's control at all times.
- Local data pool entries (iOS and Android): an optional, off-by-default feature that records four fields per completed ride or food order - the date, a keyed tag standing for the city, whether it was a ride or an order, and the tag version. Entries never leave the device and are never transmitted to Curb or any processor. They are deleted automatically once older than 90 days, the store is capped at 2,000 entries with the oldest replaced beyond that, and switching the feature off deletes every entry and destroys the device-held key that produced the tags, making prior tags permanently unreadable. The pool screen also states the number of entries held and the space they occupy, and carries a button that deletes every entry immediately without switching the feature off. Because no copy of this data exists outside the device, deletion there is deletion everywhere.

## 6. Deletion and disposal method

Deletion is immediate and permanent rather than a soft flag. When a record is deleted:

- Backend records are removed from Cloudflare Durable Object storage by key deletion, which removes the value from the durable store and from subsequent reads. No shadow copy, archive table or deleted-items store is maintained.
- A legacy Plaid access token is deleted from Curb's storage only after Plaid confirms the link is removed, so the credential can no longer be used to access the consumer's financial institution.
- Data held by a processor is removed through that processor's deletion facility. Stripe payment method detachment is performed when a rider removes a card.
- Curb maintains no offline media, no physical records and no removable storage, so physical destruction procedures do not apply. All storage is cloud-hosted and encrypted at rest.

## 7. Consumer rights and requests

Curb honours access, deletion, correction, export and opt-out requests from consumers, including those arising under the CCPA/CPRA and comparable state privacy laws.

- Self-service. Any user can delete their Curb account from the Account screen. Deletion is refused while money is still owed or a ride payment is unresolved, and the app says so. A rider can remove a saved payment method from the Payment screen. Any user can clear on-device data by deleting the app, and can delete every local data pool entry from the pool screen itself.
- Written requests. Requests may be sent to support.curb@tkhub.app and are answered within 2 business days. Curb deletes everything held except records subject to the legal retention requirement in Section 5. An authorised agent may submit on a consumer's behalf with written permission. No user is treated differently for exercising a right.
- Curb does not sell personal information, does not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, and contains no advertising, analytics or attribution SDKs.

## 8. Security controls protecting retained data

- All data is encrypted at rest across all storage systems.
- All data in transit is protected with TLS 1.2 or higher.
- Secrets and API credentials are held in the platform secret store and are never committed to source control or embedded in the mobile application.
- Access to production systems is limited to the sole operator and protected by multi-factor authentication on every underlying provider account.
- Service-to-service authentication uses scoped API tokens rather than shared static credentials.
- Data obtained through a legacy Plaid link is used solely to identify a driver's former payout bank. It is not used for any secondary purpose, not enriched, not resold and not shared with third parties.

## 9. Review

This policy is reviewed by the owner at least annually, and additionally before any release that changes what data Curb collects, where it is stored, or how long it is kept. The review confirms that the retention limits in Section 5 remain implemented in code and that no new data category has been introduced without being added to this policy. The published privacy policy is updated in the same pass so that the two remain consistent.

## 10. Incident handling

If Curb becomes aware of unauthorised access to personal data, the owner will investigate immediately, revoke and rotate affected credentials, and notify affected consumers and any affected partner, including Plaid, without undue delay and in accordance with applicable law.
